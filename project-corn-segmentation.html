Vision research · Instance segmentation

3D-to-2D Corn Plant Segmentation

2025 – 2026 Kansas State University · FarmsLab First author

Overview

A comparative study of four instance-segmentation architectures run on the MaizeField3D dataset. The training images are synthetically generated by projecting 3D terrestrial laser scans (TLS) of field-grown maize plants onto a simulated RealSense D457 camera orbiting each plant on a hemisphere — 75 views per plant, 39,000 images total across 520 plants. The study evaluates each model under both a 2-class setting (stem, leaf) and a 17-class setting that treats every leaf as its own category.

The 3D-to-2D pipeline is a 75× annotation-efficiency gain over per-image labeling: each plant is labeled once in 3D, and every instance identity propagates automatically to all 75 projected views.

What I did

  • Built the PLY-to-projected-image pipeline: camera intrinsics modeled on an Intel RealSense D457, hemisphere orbit of 5 azimuths × 5 elevations × 3 distances per plant, painter's-algorithm depth sorting, and per-instance integer masks exported to COCO polygon format.
  • Trained and benchmarked four architectures on the same dataset and splits:
    • YOLOv8m-seg — single-stage CNN
    • YOLO11m-seg (26m) — single-stage CNN with attention
    • Mask R-CNN — two-stage CNN
    • EoMT (DINOv2-S) — encoder-only transformer, query-based segmentation
  • Ran each model under both a 2-class (stem, leaf) and a 17-class (stem + leaf1–leaf16) configuration.
  • Evaluated on a held-out 78-plant test split (5,850 images) using unified COCO mask mAP@50, mAP@50–95, and recall@100 — both overall and per-class, so leaf-emergence-order performance is comparable across models.
  • Wrote the dataset-preparation, evaluation-protocol, and per-model methodology into the repository so the benchmark is reproducible.
